# Plaza of Origins at golden hour

**Start with the spawn view. Quiet the paving, break up the lamp rows, and give the armillary a clear silhouette against the sky.** In the saved captures, the stone seams and lamp posts repeat all the way to the horizon. They compete with the monument, whose upper rings are cropped at spawn on both desktop and phone. The warm sky is already there. What the plaza needs is a clearer hierarchy and a visible difference between surfaces in sunlight and surfaces in shade. ## 1. Town composition and materials

MiHoYo’s Unite talk describes a **PBR-based environment with material responses adjusted for art direction**. Haoyu Cai’s GDC talk shows separate character and environment rendering, terrain-tint authoring, grass placement, and layered material treatment for rocks. That gives you a sound model for pairing MToon actors with stylized PBR scenery. The public evidence supports those techniques. Exact trim layouts and vertex-channel assignments for individual towns remain uncertain. The town readings below are visual inferences from footage and developer material. I could verify HoYoverse’s Star Rail GDC design talk and official production material, while the requested CEDEC toon-rendering and scene-lighting sessions remain unverified. [MiHoYo at Unite Seoul 2020](https://image.docswell.com/s/UnityJapan/KWRPQ5-210617-unity-dojo20211mihoyozhenzhongyi), [Genshin GDC 2021 slides](https://tajourney.games/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/2023021315023060.pdf), [Star Rail at GDC 2025](https://gdcvault.com/play/1035480/-Honkai-Star-Rail-Reimagining)

**Apply this as a layout edit first.** Keep all eight routes legible. Make one approach dominant through width, uninterrupted stone, or a stronger border. Move the visible lamps into pairs at thresholds and small groups by benches or gates. Leave long gaps on the connecting paths. Shift one foreground tree until its canopy frames the armillary, then lift the spawn camera target slightly or test a small pullback to get the upper ring into frame. Judge this from the walking camera and phone crop. Nintendo’s *Breath of the Wild* art segment and Zelda art masterclass are relevant visual references. The *Tears of the Kingdom* GDC session is chiefly about physics and sound. **Make the floor read as a few large shapes.** The existing shader already varies stone color, but subtracts up to 26% for grout and another 7% for its wider surround. Start with **5–8% grout**, roughly **2–3 m staggered slabs** on the main approach, and roughness around **0.55–0.70** on broad stone. Keep stronger seams at curbs and steps. Concentrate brass at the pool surround and junctions. Try cream stone `#DCCBB0`, muted blue-grey trim `#727F88`, and honey brass `#BC874A` as starting sRGB swatches. Put the finest contrast at a few thresholds and keep open walking space around the pool. [Current floor shader](../../../components/world/scene/HubPlaza.tsx:139)

For the next asset pass, paint broad hue shifts, wear, and bevel accents into a shared stone-and-brass atlas. A **trim sheet** lets steps, curbs, pool lips, and cornices share the same bands of edge detail. Give color textures `SRGBColorSpace` and mask textures `NoColorSpace`. Keep the existing baked irradiance in `COLOR_0`. Use a separate attribute or packed data texture for AO, wear, and material blending where needed. Shared textures help memory. To save draws, the pieces also need to share a material and be merged or instanced. [Insomniac’s *The Ultimate Trim*, GDC](https://gdcvault.com/play/1022323/The-Ultimate-Trim-Texturing-Techniques), [three.js material docs](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/MeshStandardMaterial.html)

## 2. Foliage and grass

Your tree shader already has spherical proxy normals, a light ramp, transmission, rim, and two speeds of wind. **Change the canopy shape and diagnose the stipple before adding another lighting term.** The lawn close-up shows stipple across the trunk as well as the canopy. The code applies screen-space dither when a tree gets close to the camera or lies between the camera and player. Separately, leaf cards have a hard `0.5` alpha cutout, and the ink effect uses a half-resolution canopy mask. Test occlusion fade, leaf coverage, and ink independently during a slow camera pan. They produce different artifacts and require different fixes. [Lawn close-up](../../../docs/ai-reviews/hub-genshin/shots/before-h17-bed.jpg), [occlusion fade](../../../components/world/fx/nearFade.ts:14), [leaf shader](../../../components/world/fx/foliage.ts:65), [ink mask](../../../components/world/fx/InkMask.tsx:15)

- **Canopy shape.** Give one tree three to five overlapping crowns with different sizes, offsets, and a few clear gaps. Use simple opaque volumes for inner mass and broader leaf silhouettes on cards around the perimeter. Bake normals pointing away from **each cluster’s center** so light describes several crowns. Keep these shading normals consistent on both sides of a card. Use the actual card normal separately if you need a two-sided transmission term. Give the interior a cool green floor, the outside a lighter green, and reserve the warm rim for the sun-facing silhouette. For wind, a dedicated vertex mask can hold bases steady and let branch tips and card edges move. Your existing red channel already carries canopy AO. [Shijia Liu’s foliage normal-transfer breakdown](https://voldenuit.io/technicalenvironment-art-spring-of-the-lily-of-the-valley-1), [Genshin foliage slides](https://tajourney.games/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/2023021315023060.pdf)
- **Cheap leaf AA on your tiers.** Start with solid silhouettes, padded atlas islands, and alpha-coverage-preserving offline mipmaps. Each mip should retain roughly the same fraction of texels above the cutoff. Shorten the occlusion fade and test a small camera correction near trunks. Keep the leaf depth and ink-mask cutouts consistent. Screen-space dither will be visible during the fade. Hashed alpha is a useful fallback for disappearing distant leaves, with grain as the tradeoff. In three.js, `alphaHash` is implemented by built-in shader chunks. Your custom foliage `ShaderMaterial` would need to include their logic or supply its own. SMAA has no temporal accumulation to clean up that grain. [Wyman and McGuire’s hashed alpha paper](https://research.nvidia.com/sites/default/files/pubs/2017-02_Hashed-Alpha-Testing/Wyman2017Hashed.pdf), [r187 alpha hash shader](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/r187/src/renderers/shaders/ShaderChunk/alphahash_pars_fragment.glsl.js)
- **Alpha-to-coverage experiment.** The scene currently renders into a composer with `multisampling={0}`. To test A2C, give the **actual scene render target** samples, try 4×, and set `alphaToCoverage: true`, `transparent: false`, and `depthWrite: true`. In the custom shader, calculate `fwidth(alpha)` before discard, then write a smoothed alpha around the cutoff. A2C converts it to sample coverage. A separate single-sample ink mask will still need a compatible cutoff. Compare **4× MSAA + A2C** against **current SMAA** for sharpness, shimmer, and GPU time before choosing a default. [Current composer](../../../components/world/WorldClient.tsx:435), [r187 alpha-test shader](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/r187/src/renderers/shaders/ShaderChunk/alphatest_fragment.glsl.js)
- **Grass.** Genshin’s GDC presentation shows terrain tint and grass filling working together. Your geometry already samples the lawn’s base color, but the floor then applies baked lighting while the blades use a different ambient and sun calculation. Match the **final lit blade root** to the **final lit lawn** first. Then add a little olive or straw toward the tip, keeping local root AO shallow. Put broad color variation across 2–5 m patches and keep individual blade variation small. Use tufts and grouped flowers at path edges, under trees, and by benches. On low, use wider blades, fewer segments, and painted midsize lawn patches beyond the nearest beds. The current presets generate **12k low, 32k medium, and 60k high-tier blades**. Shrinking distant blades in the shader leaves the vertex work in place, so reduce the actual instance count when testing a cheaper tier. [Current grass shader and counts](../../../components/world/scene/HubPlaza.tsx:508), [*Ghost of Tsushima* procedural grass, GDC](https://gdcvault.com/play/1027033/)

## 3. Sky, cloud sea, and distant layers

Genshin’s GDC material shows **authored cloud silhouettes and distinct body and contour lighting combined into dynamic colored clouds**. The Unite material documents time-of-day and weather-dependent lighting. A zenith/horizon/sunward ramp is a good web translation that fits the system you already have. Butteruni’s independent capture analysis is useful evidence for layered sky and fog in one later Genshin scene. Its findings apply to that capture. [Genshin GDC cloud slides](https://tajourney.games/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/2023021315023060.pdf), [Butteruni’s capture analysis](https://butteruni.github.io/2026/09/27/genshin-rendering-analysis/)

Build the view in layers so there is a reason to look past the railing:

1. **Near cloud banks, 45–100 m out and below deck height.** Give the existing world-space cloud sea a few deliberate billows at different heights. Reuse its draw or fold it into the sky shader with world-space ray–plane intersections. Move these banks slowly so a player walking around the rim gets parallax. The current cloud-sea disc sits at `y = -40` and uses a fixed sun direction. Give it the live day-state colors and direction. [Current cloud sea](../../../components/world/scene/HubPlaza.tsx:839)
2. **Mid-distance islands, around 90–180 m.** Use two or three original silhouettes, perhaps a broken terrace, a narrow rock spire, and a low ridge. Batch them as opaque low-poly meshes under one material. Let clouds hide the bases. Leave a quiet patch of sky behind the armillary rings on the main approach.
3. **Far ridge and cirrus, from around 250 m outward.** Paint almost-flat silhouettes into the dome or its panorama. Drop their contrast and saturation toward the horizon. Use a muted periwinkle away from the sun and peach haze toward it. *Sky: Children of the Light* is a reference for using light and cloud masses to frame the experience. Guerrilla’s SIGGRAPH cloud talk is a technical reference for density and light transport. [*Art of Sky*, GDC](https://gdcvault.com/play/1026903), [*Horizon Zero Dawn* cloudscapes, SIGGRAPH](https://d3d3g8mu99pzk9.cloudfront.net/AndrewSchneider/The-Real-time-Volumetric-Cloudscapes-of-Horizon-Zero-Dawn.pdf)

For the **one-draw sky**, replace some of the repeated five-octave FBM with a mipmapped cloud atlas. Pack a soft silhouette distance, interior shade, and thickness into its channels. At each of two virtual planes, sample one cloud field with its own drift speed and scale. Fade the intersection as the ray approaches parallel to the plane, and clamp the valid distance. A near card can use the same atlas and math:

```glsl
// Positive distance inside the cloud. All colors are linear.
float aa = max(fwidth(sdf), 0.002);
float cover = smoothstep(-aa, aa, sdf);
float edge = 1.0 - smoothstep(0.02, 0.18, abs(sdf));
float towardSun = pow(max(dot(rayDir, sunDir), 0.0), 6.0);
vec3 cloud = mix(cloudShade, cloudLit, interior);
cloud += sunTint * edge * towardSun * 0.25;
sky = mix(sky, cloud, cover);
```

If you want a raymarch comparison, try **four fixed samples through a shallow horizon slab** on medium, one mipmapped density lookup per step, and front-to-back opacity accumulation. Keep the rest of the sky on the atlas path and the low preset on two planes. Also test drawing the opaque sky after opaque geometry with depth testing on and depth writes off. It currently draws early, so the shader can run behind foreground objects. [Current dome shader and order](../../../components/world/scene/HubPlaza.tsx:56)

## 4. Height fog and aerial perspective

Fog is a blend between the surface’s light and the light scattered into the view along the camera ray. **Put most of it off-island and below the plaza.** Start fading the near cloud banks and islands around 45–60 m, with stronger attenuation at low elevations. Keep the character, the pool, and most of the 72 m island crisp. Genshin’s documented console pipeline uses region-controlled, lit volumetric fog and a separate occluded sun-ray pass. A directional fog tint and a few painterly shafts concentrated on the horizon fit this budget. [Unite fog and god-ray slides](https://image.docswell.com/s/UnityJapan/KWRPQ5-210617-unity-dojo20211mihoyozhenzhongyi), [SIGGRAPH atmospheric rendering course](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2019/)

A cheap starting point is an exponential response to distance with a midpoint height approximation:

```glsl
// rayW is fragment world position minus camera world position.
float d = max(length(rayW), 0.001);
float yMid = cameraPosition.y + 0.5 * rayW.y;
float density = mix(0.012, 0.003, smoothstep(-25.0, 8.0, yMid));
float fog = 1.0 - exp(-density * max(d - 45.0, 0.0));
float warm = pow(max(dot(rayW / d, sunDir), 0.0), 5.0) * sunAmount;
rgb = mix(rgb, mix(fogAway, fogSunward, warm), fog);
```

In `floorMaterial()` and `bakedPBR`, extend the existing `onBeforeCompile`. Remove the stock `<fog_fragment>` for patched materials and insert the blend **before** `<tonemapping_fragment>`. In r187, the stock fog chunk comes **after** color-space conversion. To get a world-space ray in a built-in material, pass `mvPosition.xyz` after `<project_vertex>` and rotate back with `transpose(mat3(viewMatrix))` for a normal rigid camera. That approach includes the instance and vertex transforms already applied. Custom shaders can use their world position directly. Reuse shared uniform objects and include the patch in `customProgramCacheKey()` where applicable. For shafts, add a soft directional lobe in the dome or bake one into a cloud-bank card. Real occlusion would need additional information. [r187 shader order](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/r187/src/renderers/shaders/ShaderLib/meshphysical.glsl.js), [current hub fog](../../../components/world/scene/HubPlaza.tsx:880)

## 5. Lighting, contact, and grading

**The floor explicitly zeros direct diffuse and replaces indirect diffuse with its bake.** A stronger `DirectionalLight` can change an MToon character or a specular highlight while leaving most of the floor’s diffuse response untouched. Start by separating three things in the floor material: indirect irradiance, static contact AO, and sun visibility. Inspect the Blender bake first. If it already includes direct sun, rebake indirect-only before adding a separate sun contribution. Also check how much the minimum baked luminance and purple shadow tint are shaping the current image. [Floor lighting override](../../../components/world/scene/HubPlaza.tsx:229), [day-night controls](../../../components/world/scene/HubNight.tsx:54)

- **Key and fill.** At the reference sunset, test a 10–18° key from the side, with warm cream light and muted sky-blue fill. Start with a shaded neutral swatch around half the scene-linear luminance of the corresponding lit swatch. Keep foliage interiors and stone recesses cool and legible. Reserve the brightest accents for the armillary’s brass edges, a narrow band of water glint, and selected gates. On MToon, tune the existing shade and rim parameters with one character in the shot. A second scene light would affect every material that evaluates it.
- **Grounding.** Pack soft static contact under the pool, trunks, benches, steps, and thresholds into one world-XZ mask sampled by the existing floor shader. A 1024² texture across 72 m gives about 7 cm per texel. Put AO on indirect light and sun visibility on the direct term. A late-afternoon cast-shadow mask can add long soft shapes with `height / tan(elevation)` as a length reference. Use it near the matching sun angle, then fade it and keep the static contact. The player already has a soft ground blob. Tune its footprint and jump fade before adding more blobs, or evaluate the player ellipse inside the ground material and retire its quad. [Existing player contact](../../../components/world/scene/Hub.tsx:305)
- **Tone mapping.** The hub’s ordinary path already uses **Neutral**. Keep it as the baseline for preserving material colors. Compare **AgX** if bright brass and portal emission need a gentler roll-off and some desaturation. Compare three.js’s **ACESFilmic** if you want a stronger filmic response, checking skin, turquoise water, and gold at matched middle grey. A custom curve earns its place if you want to control the shadow toe and highlight shoulder independently. Genshin’s Unite talk discusses a custom HDR grading and tone curve, including why the full ACES HDR pipeline did not suit that presentation. [Current tone mapping](../../../components/world/WorldClient.tsx:446), [Khronos PBR Neutral](https://github.com/KhronosGroup/ToneMapping/tree/main/PBR_Neutral), [Unite HDR grading slides](https://image.docswell.com/s/UnityJapan/KWRPQ5-210617-unity-dojo20211mihoyozhenzhongyi)
- **Post.** Feed bloom scene-linear HDR. Swap the current threshold of `1.05` for a trial around `1.2–1.5` and compare a lower intensity around `0.2–0.35` against the current `0.6`. Judge the result by what crosses the luminance threshold. It should be mostly deliberate glints and emissive details. Apply tone mapping once, and apply any LUT in the domain in which it was authored. A display-referred LUT belongs after the tone curve with the expected color-space conversion. Aim for a restrained warm-highlight/cool-shadow grade and, if needed, about 3–5% corner darkening. Fold the grade into a compatible existing effect pass. Compare the ink at reduced strength or distance on environment edges, particularly railings, water rims, and leaf gaps. [React Postprocessing bloom docs](https://react-postprocessing.docs.pmnd.rs/effects/bloom)

## 6. Mirror pool and fountains

Medium and low already disable real planar reflections. The pool already has a depth LUT, Fresnel sky fallback, foam, glints, and separate ripple and foam simulations. **The cheapest visual upgrade is to make the center calmer and make the reflected sky match the visible sky.** Share the day-state sky colors, use a lower-frequency normal field through the center, and keep stronger disturbance near jet landings and the pool edge. Use a pale jade shallows color and a darker jade body. Define depth from the known basin profile or a small baked depth map. Begin foam with a thin, broken SDF rim, then add rings around jet landings. Keep scrolling caustics faint, shallow, and absent under dense foam. [Hub reflection setup](../../../components/world/scene/HubWater.tsx:219), [current pool shader](../../../components/world/fx/water.ts:278)

For a convincing **armillary reflection without a second scene render**, draw a low-detail offline proxy into a small premultiplied RGBA texture. Treat it as a vertical card through the monument. In the water fragment shader, intersect `reflect(-V, N)` from the water point with that card, mask UVs outside the card, and blend the result into the sky reflection before Fresnel. Keep it soft over the main approach. A single card will show its approximation when viewed from the side, so fade it at glancing card angles. A baked cubemap is simpler for all-around sky and distant scenery. The proxy gives the central object better local parallax at the cost of a texture sample.

For fountains, fold caustic color into the existing basin material, instance matching curtains, and use analytic landing rings. On low, test replacing decorative foam and ripple simulation steps with a fixed set of analytic rings. Keep player-triggered ripples if they provide feedback. *TUNIC* is a useful production example of using precomputed light, simple geometry, batching, and scrolling textures for local magic and atmosphere. [Andrew Shouldice’s *TUNIC* rendering interview](https://d-data.ro/smoke-mirrors-and-scrolling-textures-behind-the-scenes-of-tunic/)

## 7. Ambient life in one draw

Use one `InstancedBufferGeometry` of camera-facing quads with one RGBA atlas and static per-instance attributes for origin, seed, lifetime, size, and sprite. Give each type a small area. Petals fall below blossom trees, seeds drift near grass, butterflies loop near flowers, and fireflies pulse after dark under the canopy. A few backlit motes near the pool suffice. Start with **48–96 instances on medium and 16–32 on low**.

```glsl
float age = fract(uTime * invLifetime + seed);
vec3 center = origin + velocity * (age * lifetime);
center.xz += drift * sin(6.283 * (age + seed));
float fade = smoothstep(0.0, 0.12, age)
           * (1.0 - smoothstep(0.78, 1.0, age));
```

Use view-space quad expansion, depth testing, and `depthWrite: false`. Premultiplied alpha with limited overlap works for both petals and small glow sprites in one batch. Simulate butterfly wingbeats with a flipbook or quad-width oscillation. Give the geometry a bounding sphere that covers the shader displacement. Update the time and night uniforms in place. No per-particle JS updates, sorting, or buffer uploads are needed. Fund the draw with a draw removed in the same change, such as a caustic overlay absorbed into its basin material.
